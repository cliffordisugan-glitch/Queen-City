Know the oldest City of the Philippines?

Cebu is widely recognized as the oldest city and first capital of the Philippines, establishing its pivotal role in the nation's history long before modern urbanization. Founded in 1565 by Spanish conquistador Miguel López de Legazpi as Villa del Santísimo Nombre de Jesús, the settlement developed around the site where European explorers led by Ferdinand Magellan had first arrived in 1521.

Welcome to Cebu: The Queen City of the South

Cebu, known as the "Queen City of the South," is the oldest city and first capital of the Philippines. Nestled in the Central Visayas region, the narrow island province is flanked by narrow coastal plains, rugged mountain ranges, and rich marine ecosystems. Renowned for its unique blend of deep-rooted heritage and modern urban dynamism, Cebu serves as the cultural, educational, and commercial heart of the central and southern Philippines.

From its origins as the thriving precolonial Rajahnate of Sugbo to the site of the 1521 arrival of Ferdinand Magellan and the Battle of Mactan, Cebu played a pivotal role in shaping Philippine history. Today, it stands as a global hub for tourism, information technology, and shipping, famous worldwide for its iconic Sinulog Festival, historic landmarks, and vibrant culinary scene led by its famed roasted pig, lechon.
